1System overview

MediCart has three controllers. The Raspberry Pi 5 is the brain: it runs the camera and person detection, the Pi dashboard and the link to the web console. The Arduino Mega 2560 drives the wheels and follows the line, but only while the Pi says the path is clear. The ESP32 reads the patient's pulse and SpO2 and sends them over Wi-Fi on its own.

2Arduino Mega 2560 pin map

All pin numbers are fixed in the firmware. Keep every signal on the same pin, or change the constant at the top of medicart.ino and upload again (section 11).

PinConnected toWhat the firmware does with it
D2Left BTS7960, forward PWMPWM 0–255. Left wheel forward when > 0.
D3Left BTS7960, reverse PWMPWM 0–255. Left wheel backward.
D4Right BTS7960, forward PWMPWM 0–255. Right wheel forward.
D5Right BTS7960, reverse PWMPWM 0–255. Right wheel backward.
D6 – D13Station outputs 1 – 8HIGH for the station the camera confirmed (QR:n from the Pi), one at a time.
D22, D23Station outputs 9, 10Same as above.
D25Buzzer (active)Solid tone = stopped by a person / emergency stop / no Pi. Short beeps = line lost.
A8 – A12IR sensors S1 – S5Read as digital. LOW = black line under that sensor.
USB-BRaspberry Pi USBCommands from the Pi and power for the Mega. Serial 0 (D0 / D1) is this link.
D0, D1Keep freeThey are the USB serial link. Anything connected here breaks uploads and the Pi link.
5V, GNDIR array, BTS logic, buzzerLogic supply and common ground (section 9).

PWM frequency: D2, D3, D5 ≈ 490 Hz (timer 3), D4 ≈ 980 Hz (timer 0), Arduino defaults. All other Mega pins are unused by the firmware.

3Motor drivers (BTS7960) and motors

One BTS7960 (IBT-2) board per wheel, differential drive: the cart turns by running the two wheels at different speeds.

ItemValueNote
Direction rule MustD2 > 0 → left wheel forward · D4 > 0 → right wheel forwardWhether D2 goes to RPWM or LPWM depends on how the motor leads are connected. What matters: D2 and D4 drive the cart forward.
Enable pins MustR_EN, L_EN tied to 5 V on both boardsThe firmware never drives them. If they float, the wheels don't turn.
Line-following speed Currentbase 50 PWM, max 50, search 50About 20 % of full PWM. Steering gain 20, max steering 40, slew 300 PWM/s.
Manual speed CurrentSerial Monitor keys 50 · Pi / web starts at 40, capped at 180Speed changes are ramped (300 PWM/s); stops are immediate.
Motor model, gearbox, wheel Ø Fill inThe tuning above was made with the current motors and wheels. New ones = retest line following (section 12).
Motor supply voltage Fill inWithin the BTS7960 range and the motors' rating.
A wheel turns the wrong way after rewiring? Swap that motor's two leads (M+ / M−) on the driver output. Don't change the code: the line-following tuning assumes the current direction rule.

4IR line sensor array

A 5-channel digital line sensor (the RoboticsBD "5-array line following sensor"), black line on a light floor. The firmware averages the positions of the sensors that see the line, smooths it over 25 ms, and steers.

ItemValueNote
Wiring MustS1→A8, S2→A9, S3→A10, S4→A11, S5→A12 · VCC 5 V · GNDDigital outputs. LOW = line seen (BLACK_LINE = true).
Orientation MustS1 on the left, S5 on the right, array across the lineCentred on the cart's middle line, at the front, ahead of the drive wheels.
Height above floor Recommendedabout 3–8 mm, all five at the same heightToo high: every sensor reads "no line". Adjust each channel's trimmer so its LED changes exactly over the line.
Distance sensor → wheel axle Fill inThe steering tuning depends on it. Keep it the same as the old body if you can; if it changes, retest and maybe retune LINE_STEERING_GAIN.
Line Currentblack on a light floorAll five on the line (a crossing) → position 0, it drives straight across. None → it turns towards the side it last saw the line for 1.5 s, then stops.
Check after mounting: open the Serial Monitor (115200), type i: with the line under the middle sensor it prints S1-S5 00100. Slide the cart left and right: the 1 must move the opposite way, one sensor at a time.

6Raspberry Pi 5

Everything connects to the Pi by USB or HDMI; the 40-pin GPIO header is not used. The Pi starts everything at power-on: auto-login, the cart screen full screen, the camera split, the robot program and the web link.

ItemValueNote
Power Must5 V 5 A (27 W) USB-C, e.g. the official Pi 5 supply or a 5 V 5 A converterWith a weaker supply the Pi 5 limits all USB ports to 600 mA in total. The Mega and the webcam then drop off: we saw exactly this ("Arduino ✗, camera ✗").
Monitor MustHDMI 0, next to the power socketOn HDMI 1 the screen may stay black after the boot logo.
Mega cable Recommendedshort, good USB-A → USB-B cable with strain reliefIf it comes loose the cart stops (safe), but the robot program then waits for it.
Cooling RecommendedPi 5 active cooler, air flow inside the bodyYOLO keeps the CPU busy all the time.
Wi-Fi Recommendedkeep the Pi's antenna area free of metal sheetsThe Vercel Drive page, calls and Firebase need Wi-Fi. If no known Wi-Fi is found, the Pi opens the setup hotspot MediCart-Cart-Setup.
Access Recommendedreachable memory card and power buttonFor updates and recovery.

7Camera (safety brake, stations, calls)

One USB webcam does three jobs: it is the safety brake (YOLO person detection), it reads the station markers, and it is the cart's camera in doctor video calls. The Pi splits it in software: /dev/video10 for YOLO, /dev/video11 for calls.

ItemValueNote
Direction Mustfacing forward, in the driving direction, levelIt is the brake: it must see the path in front of the cart.
Field of view Mustnothing of the body, screen or cables in the pictureA person very close (box over 60 % of the picture) counts as STOP whatever the distance says.
Mount Mustrigid, no shaking, not blockedA covered camera is not detected as a fault yet: YOLO sees "no people" (we tested it). Keep it where it can't be blocked by a hand or an object on the cart.
Height Recommendedchest height of an adult, or as high as the body allowsSo that a standing adult at about 1 m fits from head to feet. Fill in the final height: ______ cm.
Resolution Current1280 × 720, YOLOv8n-pose, confidence 0.35Station markers: ArUco DICT_4X4_50 IDs 1–10, or QR codes.
Callsthe same camera; speaker and microphone on the PiFace the patient side as well as possible. A forward camera at chest height works for a standing or sitting patient in front of the cart.
New camera, lens, height or angle → calibrate again. Run the original tool opencv_human_distance_arucoqr.py, have a person stand at a measured distance, press C in the camera window, type the person's height and the distance. It saves camera_calibration.json, which medicart.py reads. Without calibration the dashboard shows "FOCAL: not calibrated" and distances are wrong. Then check: stand at 1 m → the cart must STOP.

8ESP32 vital sensor

A separate unit: the ESP32 reads a UART pulse-oximeter module and sends heart rate and SpO2 over its own Wi-Fi to the web console and the cart screen. It has no wire to the Pi or the Mega; it only needs 5 V power.

ItemValueNote
Board MustESP32 Dev Module (classic ESP32)Upload with board "ESP32 Dev Module", not "ESP32S3". On WROVER modules GPIO 16/17 are taken by PSRAM: use a WROOM module.
Oximeter UART Currentmodule TX → GPIO16 · module RX → GPIO17 · 115200 baudPackets 0x41 0x42 + 12 bytes.
Oximeter power3V3 (or 5 V if the module needs it) · common GNDWithout a common GND no data arrives ("No data from oximeter").
Buttons / LEDBOOT = GPIO0 (hold 5 s → Wi-Fi setup), LED = GPIO2LED: fast blink = setup, slow = connecting, solid = online.
Power5 V via USBFrom the cart's 5 V supply or a USB port. Fill in source: ______
Finger sensor placement Recommendedeasy to reach for a sitting or lying patient; cable with strain relief; the sensor itself must not moveMovement spoils SpO2; the firmware holds the last good value up to 10 s.
Antenna Recommendedkeep the ESP32's PCB antenna out of metal boxesWi-Fi setup hotspot: MediCart-Sensor-XXXX.

Monitor, speakerper device
  • Must Common ground: Mega GND, both BTS7960 logic GNDs and the IR array GND are connected. The motor battery's negative and the logic ground meet at one point.
  • Must Pi supply able to give 5 A continuously, also when the motors start (voltage dips reset the Pi or drop the USB devices).
  • Recommended Separate DC-DC converters for the Pi and for everything else, a fuse on the motor battery, and a main switch.
  • Recommended A physical emergency-stop button that cuts motor power. Today the stops are in software (Pi, web, Arduino timeout); for a hospital cart a hardware one is worth adding.
  • Recommended Keep motor wires away from the IR array, USB cables and the camera cable, and twist each motor pair.

10Safety the hardware must keep

These are built into the software. The new body must not get around them.

RuleWhat the hardware must make sure of
The Arduino stops the motors 750 ms after the last command from the PiThe motors are only driven through the Mega pins D2–D5: no second controller, switch or remote drives the BTS inputs.
The cart starts stopped after every power-onNothing makes the wheels turn at power-up (enable pins at 5 V only; PWM inputs not pulled HIGH).
A person within 1 m, or the camera / YOLO not running → STOP, in Auto and ManualThe camera sees the path ahead, is rigid and calibrated (section 7).
Line following only while the Pi says the path is clearThe USB link Pi ↔ Mega is solid; a loose cable stops the cart.
Speeds: line following 50 PWM, manual max 180 PWMNew motors or wheels with a different speed per PWM → test the stopping distance again.
Emergency stop latches until someone chooses Auto or ManualAdd the hardware E-stop (section 9) for a hospital setting.
No camera brake in the Serial Monitor tests. p (manual keys) and l (line following without the Pi) are for the bench. Keep someone next to the cart, and lift the wheels for the first tests.
